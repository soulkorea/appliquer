# Appliquer Evaluation Rubric

> **Version:** Brain v0.1.0
> **Status:** Active

## Purpose

The Evaluation Rubric defines how Appliquer evaluates career opportunities.

Its purpose is not to calculate a match score.

Its purpose is to produce a transparent, evidence-based recommendation that helps users decide whether an opportunity is worth pursuing.

Every recommendation must be explainable, repeatable, and supported by evidence.


---

## Evaluation Levels

Each dimension is evaluated using one of five levels.

| Rating | Meaning |
|----------|----------|
| Excellent | Strong evidence of alignment |
| Good | Mostly aligned with minor gaps |
| Moderate | Mixed alignment |
| Limited | Significant gaps exist |
| Poor | Major mismatch |

---

## Evidence Requirements

Every evaluation must distinguish between:

### Evidence

Facts supported by the resume or job description.

Example:

"The resume demonstrates ownership of multiple product launches."

---

### Inference

Reasonable conclusions drawn from available evidence.

Example:

"This experience is likely transferable to B2B SaaS."

---

### Assumptions

Information that is unknown.

Example:

"The job requires SQL proficiency, but the resume does not mention SQL."

---

## Recommendation Categories

Appliquer produces one of four recommendations.

### APPLY

Strong alignment.

The opportunity is likely worth pursuing.

---

### APPLY IF

Good opportunity, but only if specific conditions are true.

Example:

- salary expectations
- interest in the industry
- willingness to relocate

---

### MAYBE

Mixed evidence.

Worth consideration, but not an obvious priority.

---

### PASS

The opportunity is unlikely to justify the user's time based on current evidence.

## Weighting

Appliquer considers multiple dimensions when evaluating opportunities.

The relative importance of each dimension may evolve as the product is validated through real-world use.

The Brain should prioritize evidence-based reasoning over rigid numerical weighting.

---

## Validation Rule

Every substantial change to the Evaluation Rubric should be validated against real job opportunities before becoming part of the Brain.

The objective is not to optimize for higher scores.

The objective is to improve decision quality.

---

Every recommendation must be explainable, evidence-based, and actionable.
